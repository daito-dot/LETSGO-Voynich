## 6. What changed scientifically

Before Issue #68, a reasonable working interpretation was that the independently replicated R1 residual token-construction core might strongly constrain the mechanism family itself.

Issue #68 weakens that interpretation.

A published reversible homophonic cipher whose codebook was deliberately designed to emit Voynich-like glyph strings can reproduce the R1 residual topology extremely well:

- `r = .883` vs ZL3b;
- `r = .900` vs IT2a;
- 60–61 of 66 signs agree;
- residual existence/reliability are strong.

Therefore:

> **R1 is a strong constraint on admissible output construction, but it is not by itself evidence against reversible cipher families.**

This is a genuine narrowing of what the earlier #58C/#58D result can be claimed to establish.

## 7. Why Naibbe still fails as a joint model

Naibbe's R1 success does not rescue the model because the missing responsibilities are qualitatively different:

### R2

Its H62 short-range correlation geometry fails the preregistered joint gate. It gets the recurrence-distance profile substantially better than simple controls in some respects, but the short-range correlation responsibility remains wrong.

### R3

Naibbe's S1 values are wrong-sign in all five frozen comparisons:

`[-0.06797, -0.03253, -0.11443, -0.11846, -0.08048]`

where the manuscript target S1 is positive.

This reinforces the prior observation that paragraph-entry specialization is a harder discriminator than broad token statistics or even some recurrence signatures.

### R4

The published decoder does not uniquely recover the normalized plaintext stream under the primary published 3%-space-removal output on all source lines.

Only `65.64%` close uniquely and exactly under the frozen no-hidden-side-information rule.

Thus the tested published implementation is not an exact inverse channel under the tournament's closure requirement.

## 8. The key mechanistic ambiguity exposed by R1 PASS

Naibbe is not a neutral historical cipher null.

Its broad cipher architecture is independently published, but its concrete glyph codebook was designed after observing Voynich structure. Consequently the R1 match can arise from at least two conceptually different sources:

1. **process effect** — homophonic/stateful encryption of plaintext naturally induces the same token-construction graph;
2. **codebook effect** — the target-aware Voynich-like output codebook already embeds enough of the 12-slot occupancy grammar that the encryption process merely samples it.

Issue #68 does not distinguish these explanations.

That distinction is now more important than discovering another individual Voynich edge.

## 9. Next frontier

The next highest-value test is a **prospectively frozen Naibbe R1 mechanism decomposition / target-dependence audit**.

Core question:

> **Does Naibbe's R1 PASS survive when the encryption architecture is held fixed but the target-aware codebook association is neutralized under controlled counterfactuals?**

This should be done at complete-graph level, not by examining which of the 66 edges differ.

The decomposition should separate at minimum:

- fixed published architecture + fixed published target-aware codebook;
- codebook-association permutation controls that preserve the published glyph inventory/table/state capacity but break letter/state-to-glyph association;
- a prospectively specified inventory-only / token-sampling control to test whether R1 is largely hard-coded in emitted token inventory rather than generated by plaintext/cipher dynamics;
- ideally a non-target-aware codebook control if a principled one can be frozen without using Issue #68 edge outcomes.

If R1 survives these neutralizations, the constraint is likely mostly a property of the output inventory/slot grammar and should be down-weighted as a process discriminator.

If R1 collapses, the published Naibbe architecture/codebook interaction is doing something materially more specific and deserves deeper reversible-family study.

R2/R3/R4 remain separate hard responsibilities regardless of this decomposition.

## 10. Interpretation boundary

Issue #68 does **not** establish that Voynich is Naibbe-like, encrypted Latin, a homophonic cipher, or meaningful plaintext.

It also does not show that A1 is historically plausible or that its native representation fails token grammar.

What it establishes is narrower and useful:

> **One target-aware reversible cipher family can reproduce the independently replicated complete token-construction core, but the same published mechanism fails the manuscript's short-range correlation geometry, paragraph-entry specialization, and exact decoder closure.**

The research should therefore treat R1 as one member of a joint constraint system, not as a standalone mechanism identifier.
