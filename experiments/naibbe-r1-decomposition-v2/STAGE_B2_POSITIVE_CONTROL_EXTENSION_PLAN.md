## 1. Why Stage B2 exists

Stage B1 showed that unchanged published Naibbe R1 geometry is highly stable across the five historical Phase64B realizations:

- pairwise complete-66 residual-Z Pearson `0.9761–0.9965`;
- pairwise sign agreement `64–66/66`;
- worst-reading target Pearson `M_R` `0.8578–0.8830`.

That establishes repeatability, but `n=5` is too small to treat the minimum, maximum, median±MAD, or any round expansion of them as a scientifically privileged equivalence boundary.

Stage B2 therefore expands the **unchanged-mechanism measurement distribution before any intervention R1 reveal**. Its purpose is calibration, not making the future test harder.

## 2. Scientific question

> **What is the prospective distribution of complete R1 geometry under additional unchanged executions of the exact published Naibbe configuration, using an outcome-independent continuation of the already frozen seed schedule?**

This is a T2 positive-control calibration question.

It does not ask whether Naibbe is historically correct, and it does not test any causal intervention.

## 8. What may later be calibrated from B2

The 25-rep population may be used prospectively as:

- an empirical scale for unchanged-mechanism variation;
- an effect-size denominator/context for intervention shifts;
- a positive-control reference distribution;
- a check that an intervention effect is larger than ordinary stochastic execution variation.

It does **not** automatically create a hard PASS/FAIL threshold.

Any later equivalence/non-inferiority boundary must state the scientific claim it implements and why the chosen tail probability or loss tolerance corresponds to that claim.

The mere fact that a value lies below the worst of 25 positives does not prove mechanism failure, and lying inside the 25-positive range does not prove equivalence.
