3. cross-unit predictive information is real but small relative to token-internal information;

8. the edge architecture and one reading-balanced common-EVA base table are stable across ZL3b/IT2a;

These are surface-production constraints. They do not prove natural-language words, plaintext, semantics, a cipher family, authorship, hoax/artificial origin, historical direction, or a historical production algorithm.

## 4. Strongest current falsifier

The strongest shared failure of the two historical anchors is **R5/R6**.

Both anchors:

- fail to produce positive useful same-line previous-terminal → next-initial gain;
- instead preserve an unwanted robust continuation across source-line breaks.

This is the opposite localization from the accepted Voynich surface structure.

Therefore R1 boundary behavior alone is weakly discriminating: two architecturally different historical anchors can pass it. A serious next mechanism must explain the line-local edge and its reset, not merely token-internal topology or boundary asymmetry.

## 5. What the current result suggests — but does not yet establish

The accepted constraints operate at several observable scales:

- bounded-unit / token-internal construction;
- adjacent-unit same-line dependence;
- source-line reset;
- paragraph-entry and prior-paragraph inventory effects;
- Currier-level global next-initial bias.

It is therefore reasonable to ask whether the historical production process had multiple operational timescales or nested state scopes. That is a **research direction**, not an accepted historical conclusion.

Do not silently convert this multiscale description into a claim that the manuscript used a particular state machine, cipher, template, copying procedure, language, or notation system.
