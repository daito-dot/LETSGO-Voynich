## What is externally established

### Concrete T0 anchor

The fol. 37v codebook in Francesco Tranchedino, ÖNB Cod. 2398 is identified in modern secondary literature as a Milanese key dated 23 August 1463 for Antonio de Besana.

The reported inventory includes:

- 21 alphabetic plaintext letters;
- the additional plaintext elements `&`, `con`, `ex`;
- 2–3 cipher equivalents for letters, with A/e/h/l/q reported as receiving three;
- 12 null/dummy signs;
- 12 dedicated doubled-letter signs;
- 63 vowel+consonant digram signs;
- 31 whole-word/name nomenclator entries;
- reported total: 165 ciphertext signs for 130 meaningful plaintext terms.

Primary descriptive source used for the folio-specific counts:

- Joachim von zur Gathen, *CryptoSchool*, section D.2/D.6: https://dokumen.pub/cryptoschool-1nbsped-3662484234-978-3662484234.html

The same source identifies T0 as fol. 37v and compares it with T1/T2/T3 from the same Tranchedino collection. The recurring plaintext categories are reported as letters/standard abbreviations, optional short words, dummies, vowel+consonant digrams, doubled consonants, and mission-specific words/names. C1–C5 are comparatively stable; C6 varies by mission.

### Manuscript/family provenance

Cod. 2398 is a real Sforza-chancery working collection, compiled around 1475 from material covering approximately 1450–1496 and later expanded. Modern facsimile descriptions report hundreds of complete/partial keys, alphabetic substitutions, digraphs/trigraphs, nomenclatures and nulls.

Sources:

- manuscript/facsimile description: https://www.facsimiles.com/facsimiles/francesco-tranchedino-diplomatic-secret-documents
- alternate facsimile description: https://www.facsimilefinder.com/facsimiles/francesco-tranchedino-secret-diplomatic-documents-facsimile
- Austrian manuscript catalogue/bibliographic authority referenced in the candidate preflight.

### Independent family-level corroboration

The DECRYPT/DECODE survey of more than 1,600 historical keys independently confirms that fifteenth-century nomenclatures can mix letters, syllables, words/names, graphic signs, Latin letters and diacritics. Syllabic/plaintext multi-letter units occur already in fifteenth-century material.

Source:

- Megyesi et al., *Keys with nomenclatures in early modern Europe*: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611194.2022.2113185

A separate instruction survey identifies another key in the same Cod. 2398 (f.10r, DECODE key ID-2373, dated broadly 1450–1496) with an explicit null instruction: material placed between two declared signs carries no plaintext value. This proves that at least some Tranchedino-family keys included operational null instructions rather than only null labels.

Source:

- Megyesi et al., *Cipher key instructions in early modern Europe: analysis and text edition*: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611194.2024.2396800

This is family-level operational evidence; it is not evidence that fol.37v used that exact instruction.

## Count reconciliation

One apparent contradiction can be resolved cleanly on the plaintext side.

CryptoSchool later states that T0 has 130 plaintext terms before adding a fictitious 11-element C2 short-word category. T0 explicitly omits C2.

Using the reported T0 categories:

- C1 letters + standard abbreviations = 21 + 3 = 24;
- C4 vowel+consonant digrams = 63;
- C5 doubled consonants = 12;
- C6 words/names = 31.

Therefore:

`24 + 63 + 12 + 31 = 130`.

The 12 dummies are correctly absent from the plaintext-term count because they encode no plaintext element.

So the reported `130 plaintext terms` is internally coherent.

## Unresolved cipher-sign arithmetic

The reported `165 ciphertext signs` still cannot be reconstructed from the prose summary without an unsupported assumption.

If the folio-specific homophone description is taken literally:

- 5 letters × 3 signs = 15;
- 16 other letters × 2 signs = 32;
- `&`, `con`, `ex` × 1 sign = 3;
- nulls = 12;
- digrams = 63;
- doubled letters = 12;
- nomenclator = 31.

This gives:

`15 + 32 + 3 + 12 + 63 + 12 + 31 = 168`,

not 165.

The three-sign discrepancy could arise from a detail not visible in the prose description — for example a different homophone multiplicity for a few elements, a collision/shared sign, a different null count in the exact table, or a counting convention. H0 found no authority strong enough to choose among those possibilities.

No arithmetic repair is licensed.

## Facsimile-access result

Public facsimile vendor pages expose multiple representative photographs of Cod. 2398 and visibly corroborate the table/nomenclator layout, but the accessible photographs are not identified as a complete, machine-verifiable fol.37v diplomatic transcription. They are insufficient to audit all 165 signs, collisions and mappings cell-by-cell.

The 1970 ADEVA facsimile with Walter Höflechner commentary remains the natural authority for a future manual/archival recovery, but H0 does not currently possess a reproducible digital fol.37v table authority.

## Encoding-policy audit

H0 found strong evidence for the **inventory** of legal plaintext elements but not a fol.37v rule that determines, for an arbitrary plaintext string, which overlapping unit must be used when several are legal.

Examples of unresolved choices include:

- single letters versus an available vowel+consonant digram;
- two single letters versus a doubled-letter sign;
- spelling a nomenclator word letter-by-letter versus using its whole-word code;
- which homophone to choose on each letter occurrence;
- where/how often to insert nulls;
- whether ordinary source spaces are retained, suppressed or otherwise recoverable in actual use.

Simonetta's surviving 1474 rules concern cryptanalysis and are not an encoder manual for T0. The general instruction literature establishes that explicit operational instructions sometimes existed, including within Cod.2398, but H0 found no authority transporting such a complete choice policy to fol.37v.

Any of the following would therefore be a modern model choice rather than a recovered T1463 mechanism:

- greedy longest-match;
- digram-first;
- nomenclator-first;
- uniform random choice among legal units;
- frequency-weighted homophone choice;
- a fixed null insertion probability.

Those choices may define legitimate new mathematical candidates in a later phase, but they may not be smuggled into `T1463-A0` as historical facts.

T1463-A0 therefore does **not** enter the #172 joint target tournament and no H1 external encoder is licensed under this issue.

## Scientific consequence

T1463 contributes a useful negative/positive boundary to the mechanism tree:

1. Fifteenth-century northern-Italian cryptography genuinely used mixed plaintext granularity, homophony, nulls, dedicated double-letter elements and mission nomenclatures. These features need not be invented to keep such cipher families historically live.
2. Historical plausibility of a component inventory is not enough to define a confirmatory generator. The usage policy is part of the mechanism and must be independently specified or charged.
3. For R2, an even stronger historical candidate would be one where the **cipher output itself has an independently documented multi-glyph internal construction**, rather than merely mapping multi-letter plaintext units to opaque single cipher signs.

The next architecture-selection step should therefore search for such an externally specified structured-output mechanism before any new Voynich scoring.
